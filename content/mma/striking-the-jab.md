# Striking: The Jab and Why It Runs Everything

## Why the jab matters

The jab is the punch thrown with your lead hand, straight down the middle. It is
rarely the punch that ends a fight, and that is exactly why it matters: it is the
cheapest strike you have. It is quick, it leaves you balanced, and it barely
opens you up. That low cost lets it do jobs no other punch can:

- **Measure distance.** Every jab tells you where the opponent is and where their hands are.
- **Interrupt.** A jab in the face breaks an opponent's rhythm and their plan to step in.
- **Set up.** It hides and creates openings for the power hand behind it.
- **Score.** Clean, consistent jabs are visible, effective strikes.

## Mechanics — and the why behind each

| Detail | Why |
|---|---|
| Start from stance, lead hand at cheek height | A jab thrown from a low hand travels further and signals itself |
| Extend straight from the chin; turn the fist over at the end | A straight line is the shortest path; turning the fist aligns the knuckles and raises the shoulder to cover the chin |
| Small push off the rear foot, lead foot can step slightly | Moves body weight behind the punch without committing to it |
| Rear hand stays glued to the face | The jab is thrown at the range where the opponent can counter |
| Return along the same line, as fast as it went out | A slow return is how you get countered with a right hand over the top |

## How it changes in MMA

MMA adds takedowns, kicks and small gloves, which changes the jab's job:

- **Stance is wider and more upright** to defend takedowns, so you have slightly less reach on the jab.
- **A lunging jab invites a level change.** If your jab pulls your weight onto the front foot, a wrestler can duck under it and shoot. Keep the jab on balance.
- **The jab defends against wrestling too.** A stiff jab lands as an opponent drops to shoot and can stop the entry.
- **Small gloves give less cover.** Your guard hand has to be more precise, and a parry has less margin.

## Drills

1. **Shadowboxing: jab only.** 3 rounds of 2–3 minutes. Vary: single, double, jab while stepping back, jab while circling.
2. **Mirror check.** Watch that the rear hand stays up and the chin stays tucked behind the lead shoulder.
3. **Partner distance game (no contact or light touch).** One partner moves; the other tries to touch the shoulder with a jab only at the right range. It teaches distance, not power.
4. **Bag or pads: jab → cross, jab → level change.** The second pattern builds the MMA habit of mixing strikes and takedown threats.

## Common errors

- Dropping the jab hand on the return — the most common way to get hit.
- Leaning forward to reach instead of stepping; it leaves you off balance and open to takedowns.
- Throwing jabs with no purpose; each should find range, interrupt, or set up.

## Safety notes

- Wrap your hands for any bag or pad work. Hand wraps protect the small bones of the hand and wrist.
- Drill with a partner at controlled, agreed intensity. Hard sparring should be supervised by a qualified coach, use proper gloves and a mouthguard, and be kept to a limited amount of your training.
- Repeated head impacts carry real long-term risk. Stop and get medically assessed if you have any concussion symptoms (headache, dizziness, confusion, sensitivity to light, nausea) — do not train through them.
