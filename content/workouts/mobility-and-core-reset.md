# Mobility and Core Reset

## Goal

Restore range of motion and trunk control on a light day without adding
fatigue. Useful the day after hard sparring or heavy lifting, or as a
standalone 30-minute session on a rest day. You should finish feeling better
than when you started.

## Equipment

- A mat or carpet
- A resistance band or a strap/belt
- Optional: a foam roller

## Warm-up (5 minutes)

1. 2 minutes of easy walking or marching in place.
2. 10 cat-cows, slow, moving one segment of the spine at a time.
3. 10 arm circles forward and back, 10 hip circles each direction.

## The session

Move slowly. Breathe out as you move into each range. Nothing here should be
painful — stretching sensation is fine, sharp or pinching pain is a stop signal.

**Block 1 — hips (2 rounds)**
- 90/90 hip switches: 8 each side
- Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch with a glute squeeze: 45 seconds each side
- Deep squat hold, holding onto a door frame if needed: 45 seconds

**Block 2 — upper back and shoulders (2 rounds)**
- Open books (side-lying thoracic rotation): 8 each side
- Band shoulder dislocates (wide grip): 10
- Thread the needle: 6 each side

**Block 3 — trunk control (3 rounds)**
- Dead bug: 8 each side, lower back pressed into the floor
- Side plank: 20–30 seconds each side
- Bird dog: 8 each side, 2-second hold

## Cool-down (3–5 minutes)

- Child's pose, 60 seconds.
- Lying on your back, knees bent, slow breathing: in through the nose, long exhale, for 2 minutes.

## Progression

This session is not meant to get harder in the usual sense. Progress by:

- Holding end ranges a little longer, or adding a slow pause at the end of each rep.
- Increasing the side plank hold toward 45 seconds, then elevating the top leg.
- Moving the dead bug to a straighter-leg variation once the lower back stays down.

If a single area stays restricted or painful for weeks despite this work, see a
physiotherapist or sports medicine clinician rather than stretching harder.
