# Home Strength — Bodyweight and Bands

## Goal

Keep strength and muscle moving forward with minimal kit. This session is built
to be progressed by *leverage and tempo* rather than by adding plates, which is
the part most home programs skip.

## Equipment

- A pull-up bar (doorway bar or park bar)
- Two or three resistance bands of different strengths
- A chair or bench for split squats
- Optional: an ab wheel

## Warm-up (8 minutes)

1. 3 minutes of jump rope or marching in place.
2. 10 scapular pull-ups: hang, then pull the shoulder blades down without bending the arms.
3. 15 band pull-aparts.
4. 10 slow bodyweight squats and 5 inchworms.

## The session

Perform as three mini-circuits. Rest about 60 seconds after each circuit round.

**Circuit 1 — upper body, 4 rounds**
- Pull-ups (or band-assisted pull-ups): as many clean reps as possible, stop 1–2 short of failure
- Push-ups: as many clean reps as possible, 2-second lowering

**Circuit 2 — legs, 3 rounds**
- Bulgarian split squat (rear foot on chair): 10–12 each leg
- Single-leg glute bridge: 12 each leg, pause 1 second at the top

**Circuit 3 — accessories and core, 3 rounds**
- Band row (anchored at chest height): 15
- Band face pull: 15–20
- Pike push-ups: 8–10
- Hollow body hold: 20–30 seconds (lower back pressed into the floor)

## Cool-down (5 minutes)

- Dead hang from the bar, 20–30 seconds, relaxed.
- Lat stretch holding the bar or a door frame, 30 seconds each side.
- World's greatest stretch, 3 slow reps each side.

## Progression

Bodyweight work progresses along four dials; turn one at a time:

1. **Reps** — add a rep per set until you reach the top of the range.
2. **Tempo** — slow the lowering to 3–4 seconds; the same rep count gets harder.
3. **Leverage** — push-ups to feet-elevated push-ups; pike push-ups with feet on a chair; split squats with a pause at the bottom.
4. **Assistance** — for pull-ups, move to a lighter band, then to negatives, then to unassisted.

When a movement becomes easy for 15+ quality reps, move to the harder variation
rather than chasing 30-rep sets; high reps turn a strength session into a
conditioning session.
