# Zone 2 Aerobic Base

## Goal

Build aerobic fitness — the system that powers recovery between efforts, between
rounds, and between training days — with a session easy enough to do often.
"Zone 2" here means an effort where you can still speak in full sentences but
would not want to sing.

## Equipment

Any one of:

- Running shoes and a route or treadmill
- A bike (road, stationary or air bike)
- A rower
- Optional: a heart-rate monitor. Not required — the talk test works

## Warm-up (5–10 minutes)

Start the same activity at a very easy pace and build over 5–10 minutes into the
target effort. Add 5 leg swings each direction and 5 walking lunges before a run
if your hips are stiff.

## The session

- **30–60 minutes continuous** at conversational effort.
- Check the talk test every 10 minutes: say a full sentence out loud. If you cannot, slow down — walking up a hill is fine.
- Keep the pace steady. If your effort drifts upward over the session (common in heat), reduce the pace to stay at the same effort rather than the same speed.
- Nasal breathing for most of the session is a useful rough guide that you are in the right zone.

The most common mistake is going too hard. Easy days that turn into medium days
make the hard days worse without making the easy days more useful.

## Cool-down (5 minutes)

- 3–5 minutes of very easy movement at the end.
- Calf, quad and hip flexor stretches, 30 seconds each side.
- Drink some water and log the session (type: cardio) with duration and distance.

## Progression

1. Increase duration first: add about 5–10 minutes per week to the longest session.
2. Then increase frequency: two sessions a week, then three.
3. Track the pace (or power) you hold at the same effort. Getting faster at the same conversational effort is the sign it is working — that is the number to watch, not how tired you feel.

If you are also doing hard conditioning, keep the zone 2 sessions genuinely
easy; they are the base that lets the hard work land.
