# Myth: "Eating Late at Night Makes You Gain Fat"

## The claim

Food eaten after a certain hour — 6 pm, 8 pm, "after dark" — is stored as fat,
while the same food eaten earlier is burned off.

## Why it is mostly wrong

Body-fat change over weeks and months is driven by energy balance: the total
energy you take in versus the total you use. Your body does not reset its
accounts at midnight. A meal eaten at 9 pm is digested and its energy used or
stored across the following hours and days, just as a meal at noon is.

Put simply: if two days contain the same food in the same amounts, moving one
of the meals to later in the evening does not make it fattening by itself.

## Why the myth feels true

People do often gain weight from late eating — but the reasons are about *what*
and *how much*, not the clock:

- **Late eating is often extra eating.** Dinner plus an evening of snacks in front of a screen adds up to more total food, and it is usually the energy-dense kind.
- **Tiredness weakens food choices.** Late at night, after a long day, decisions tend to drift toward convenient, high-calorie options.
- **The scale lies in the morning.** A large late meal means more food, fluid and salt still in your system at your morning weigh-in. That is weight, not fat, and it passes.

## Where timing *does* matter

The myth is wrong about fat, but timing is not irrelevant:

- **Sleep.** A very large or rich meal right before bed can cause reflux or disturb sleep for some people. Poor sleep affects appetite and training quality.
- **Training fuel.** If you train in the evening, eating dinner afterwards is a sensible time to eat, not a problem to avoid.
- **Consistency.** A regular meal pattern you can keep up beats an ideal schedule you cannot.
- **Medical situations.** People managing conditions such as diabetes may have specific timing advice from their clinician; follow that, not general guidance.

## What to do instead

- Judge your eating by the whole day and week, not by the time on the clock.
- If evening snacking is the issue, plan it: a portioned snack you chose earlier beats an open bag.
- Keep protein and vegetables in your evening meal so you are not hungry again at 10 pm.
- If late meals disturb your sleep, finish larger meals a couple of hours before bed.

Use the weekly trend on the Weight page, not one morning's reading, to judge
whether a change is working.
