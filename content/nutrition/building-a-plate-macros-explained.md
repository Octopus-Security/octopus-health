# Building a Plate — Macros Explained

"Macros" are the three nutrients that supply most of the energy in food:
protein, carbohydrate and fat. The Meals page in this app tracks all three
alongside calories. This piece explains what each one is *for*, so the numbers
mean something when you log them.

## The three macronutrients

**Protein** — the building material. Your body uses it to repair and build
muscle and other tissue, and it tends to be the most filling of the three per
calorie. Sources: meat, fish, eggs, dairy, tofu and tempeh, beans and lentils,
protein powder.

**Carbohydrate** — the fuel most readily available for hard, fast efforts
(sprints, heavy sets, sparring rounds). Stored in muscle and liver as glycogen.
Sources: rice, potatoes, oats, bread, pasta, fruit, beans. Vegetables contribute
carbohydrate too, plus fibre.

**Fat** — needed for hormone production, cell membranes and absorbing the
fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). The most energy-dense of the three. Sources:
olive oil, nuts and nut butters, avocado, cheese, oily fish, eggs.

On nutrition labels, protein and carbohydrate are counted at about 4 calories
per gram and fat at about 9 — which is why a spoonful of peanut butter moves the
calorie total far more than a spoonful of rice.

## A hand-sized plate

Weighing every ingredient is accurate but hard to sustain. A practical
alternative (popularised by coaching programmes as the "hand portion" method)
uses your own hand as the measuring tool, because it scales roughly with your
body size and is always with you:

| Component | Portion guide | Per main meal |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | Your palm (thickness and width) | 1–2 palms |
| Vegetables | Your fist | 1–2 fists |
| Carbohydrate | Your cupped hand | 1–2 cupped hands |
| Fat | Your thumb | 1–2 thumbs |

Start in the middle of those ranges and adjust by results over a couple of
weeks, not by one day's weigh-in:

- Losing weight faster than intended, or training feels flat: add a cupped hand of carbohydrate around training.
- Not moving toward a fat-loss goal: take away a thumb of fat or a cupped hand of carbohydrate from one or two meals.
- Always hungry: check protein and vegetables first — they add volume and fullness.

## Timing, briefly

Total intake across the day matters far more than exact timing. The practical
exceptions: eat a carbohydrate-containing meal a few hours before hard
training so you are fuelled, and include protein in the meal after training
because it is convenient and you are likely hungry.

## Where this stops

This is general guidance for healthy adults. Specific medical conditions
(diabetes, kidney disease, eating disorders), pregnancy, and making weight for a
competition are all cases for a registered dietitian, not a portion chart.
