# Progression Ladder: Pike and Handstand Push-ups

This is the overhead-pressing line. The pike push-up shifts more of your
bodyweight onto your shoulders as your hips rise and your feet go up, until
you're in a handstand.

**Before the wall work:** learn to get out of a handstand safely. Practise
walking your hands down and stepping or cartwheeling out, on a clear floor
with space around you.

## The ladder

### 1. Pike push-up
- **Equipment:** floor (or push-up handles)
- **How:** Start in a push-up position, then walk your feet in and lift your hips high so your body makes an upside-down V. Bend your elbows to lower the top of your head toward a point just in front of your hands, forming a tripod with your hands. Press back up.
- **Common faults:** head going down between the hands instead of in front of them; elbows flaring straight out; hips dropping so it becomes a push-up.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10.
- **Regression:** less pike (feet further back), or a shorter range.

### 2. Elevated pike push-up
- **Equipment:** chair or box for the feet (optional); floor or push-up handles
- **How:** Put your feet on the chair and your hands on the floor, with your hips stacked above your shoulders as far as you can. Do pike push-ups.
- **Common faults:** hips drifting back so the torso isn't vertical; the chair sliding (brace it against a wall).
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 with your torso close to vertical.
- **Regression:** pike push-ups on the floor.

### 3. Deficit pike push-up on handles
- **Equipment:** push-up handles; chair or box for the feet (optional)
- **How:** Do the elevated pike with your hands on the handles, so your head can go below hand level for extra range.
- **Common faults:** crashing into the bottom; losing shoulder stacking.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 at full controlled depth.
- **Regression:** elevated pike without the deficit.

### 4. Wall handstand hold
- **Equipment:** a clear wall; push-up handles (optional)
- **How:** Use one of two entries. For chest-to-wall, walk your feet up the wall as your hands walk in, so your chest faces the wall. For back-to-wall, kick up with your back to the wall. Arms locked, shoulders pushed up toward your ears (elevated in the handstand), and body tight. Hold.
- **Common faults:** a banana-shaped arch in the lower back; bent arms; no plan to come down.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 30-second holds, with a controlled exit each time.
- **Regression:** a pike hold with your feet on a chair (optional) for time.

### 5. Wall handstand push-up negative
- **Equipment:** a clear wall; a folded cushion or mat under your head (optional, recommended)
- **How:** From the wall handstand, lower slowly (3–5 seconds) until the top of your head touches the cushion. Come down off the wall and reset. Don't press back up yet.
- **Common faults:** dropping onto the head; elbows flaring out wide; arching.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 lowerings of 5 seconds each.
- **Regression:** deficit pike push-ups.

### 6. Partial-range wall handstand push-up
- **Equipment:** a clear wall; stacked cushions or a folded mat (optional)
- **How:** Lower to a raised cushion stack, pause, then press back to lockout. Lower the stack over time.
- **Common faults:** bouncing off the cushion; feet sliding on the wall.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 to a given height. Then lower the stack and repeat until you reach full range.
- **Regression:** negatives only.

### 7. Full wall handstand push-up
- **Equipment:** a clear wall; a cushion for the head (optional)
- **How:** Lower until your head lightly touches the floor or cushion, forming a tripod with your hands, then press to full lockout.
- **Common faults:** only locking out halfway; neck taking load because you drop onto it.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5.
- **Regression:** partial-range reps.

### 8. Deficit wall handstand push-up on handles
- **Equipment:** a clear wall; push-up handles
- **How:** Place the handles against the wall and do handstand push-ups on them, so your head can go below hand level.
- **Common faults:** going deeper than you can press out of; handles slipping.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 at full deficit depth. This is the top of this ladder; the next step is freestanding handstand work, which is a separate skill.
- **Regression:** full wall handstand push-ups on the floor.

## Safety notes

- **Neck:** never let bodyweight drop onto your head. A cushion helps, but control is what protects you. Stop immediately if you feel neck pain.
- **Bail first:** know how to come down from every handstand position before you load it.
- **Wrists:** warm up with wrist circles and gentle rocking in a kneeling position. The handles reduce wrist extension, but make sure they can't slip on the floor.
- People with high blood pressure, glaucoma, or other conditions affected by being upside down should get medical advice before inverted work.
