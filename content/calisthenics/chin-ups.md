# Progression Ladder: Chin-ups

Underhand grip (palms toward you), hands about shoulder-width. Chin-ups
usually feel easier than pull-ups because the biceps help more. That makes
them a good first full pulling rep, and a good second pulling exercise later.

## The ladder

### 1. Flexed-arm hang (underhand)
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; chair or box (optional)
- **How:** Step up to the top position, palms toward you. Keep your chest close to the bar, chin over it, shoulders down. Hold.
- **Common faults:** chin tipped up over the bar; shrugging.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 20-second holds.
- **Regression:** hold at elbows 90°, or with light foot support.

### 2. Negative chin-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; chair or box (optional)
- **How:** Start at the top and lower yourself evenly to straight arms in 5 seconds or more. Step back up.
- **Common faults:** dropping through the last third; shrugging at the bottom.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 lowerings of 5 seconds each.
- **Regression:** 3-second lowerings, or the flexed-arm hang.

### 3. Feet-assisted chin-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; chair or box (optional)
- **How:** Do a full-range chin-up, pushing lightly through your feet on the chair. Use less assistance each session.
- **Common faults:** legs doing the work; skipping the straight-arm bottom.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 with light foot pressure.
- **Regression:** more leg help, or negatives.

### 4. Full chin-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** From an active hang, pull until your chin clears the bar with your chest close to it. Lower to straight arms.
- **Common faults:** half reps; swinging; elbows flaring wide.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10 strict.
- **Regression:** feet-assisted chin-ups for back-off sets.

### 5. Pause chin-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** Do a full chin-up with a 2-second pause at the top and another halfway down.
- **Common faults:** losing height during the pause; rushing the lowering between pauses.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 6 with both pauses.
- **Regression:** a single pause at the top only.

### 6. Weighted chin-up (optional load)
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; backpack with books or water bottles (optional)
- **How:** Do strict chin-ups wearing a backpack. Add a small amount of load at a time, and pack it tightly so it doesn't swing.
- **Common faults:** adding load before bodyweight reps are solid; shortening the range.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 6 with the added load. Then add a little more load.
- **Regression:** remove the load and go back to pause chin-ups.

## Safety notes

- The underhand grip loads the biceps and the inner elbow more. Alternate chin-up days with pull-up days rather than doing high volume of both.
- If the front of your elbow or upper arm aches, cut volume, and drop the pauses and the load before anything else.
- Keep optional load secure. A shifting backpack can swing you into the door frame.
