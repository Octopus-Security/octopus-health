# Progression Ladder: Legs

Bodyweight leg training gets harder mainly by moving to **one leg at a time**.
This file has two lines. **The squat line** is knee-dominant. **The hinge line**
works the hamstrings and glutes, which squats alone under-train. Use the dip
bars or the pull-up bar's frame or post as a handhold for balance on the
single-leg rungs.

## The ladder

### 1. Bodyweight squat
- **Equipment:** none
- **How:** Feet about shoulder-width, toes turned out slightly. Sit your hips back and down, knees tracking over your toes, to at least thighs-parallel. Stand up fully.
- **Common faults:** knees caving in; heels lifting; stopping short of depth.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 20 with full depth.
- **Regression:** squat to a chair (optional) and stand back up.

### 2. Split squat
- **Equipment:** none; a dip bar for balance (optional)
- **How:** Take a long stride stance. Lower your back knee toward the floor, keeping your torso tall and front heel down. Drive up through the front foot.
- **Common faults:** front knee caving in; pushing off the back foot; short range.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12 each leg.
- **Regression:** a shorter range, holding a dip bar for balance.

### 3. Bulgarian split squat
- **Equipment:** chair, box or step for the rear foot (optional); dip bar for balance (optional)
- **How:** Put the top of your rear foot on the chair behind you. Lower until your front thigh is about parallel to the floor, then drive up through the front leg.
- **Common faults:** standing too close to the chair; the rear leg doing the work; torso collapsing forward.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12 each leg, then add a 2-second pause at the bottom until 3 × 10 paused is solid.
- **Regression:** split squat.

### 4. Shrimp squat
- **Equipment:** a cushion under the back knee (optional); dip bar for balance (optional)
- **How:** Stand on one leg and hold the other foot behind you, knee bent. Lower until the back knee touches the cushion, then stand up on the front leg.
- **Common faults:** crashing onto the knee; falling forward; the standing heel lifting.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 each leg with a light knee touch. Then remove the cushion.
- **Regression:** hold a dip bar for assistance, or use a higher cushion stack.

### 5. Pistol squat
- **Equipment:** dip bars or pull-up bar post for assistance at first; a chair (optional)
- **How:** Balance on one leg with the other leg straight out in front. Sit all the way down and stand back up. Build in stages:
  1. Hold a support and lower to a chair.
  2. Hold a support to full depth.
  3. No support.
- **Common faults:** heel lifting; the knee caving in; collapsing at the bottom; the free leg dropping and touching.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 each leg unassisted. This is the top of the squat line; progress with tempo and pauses, or optional load in a backpack.
- **Regression:** a pistol to a chair, or an assisted pistol holding a bar.

### 6. Glute bridge
- **Equipment:** none
- **How:** Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. Drive through your heels to lift your hips until your body is straight from shoulders to knees. Squeeze your glutes for 1 second, then lower.
- **Common faults:** arching your lower back instead of extending at the hips; pushing through your toes.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 20.
- **Regression:** a smaller range.

### 7. Single-leg glute bridge
- **Equipment:** none; your upper back on a chair or couch for the hip-thrust version (optional)
- **How:** Do the bridge on one leg, with the other knee pulled toward your chest. Keep your hips level.
- **Common faults:** hips twisting; hamstrings cramping (move the foot closer to you).
- **Move up when:** 3 × 15 each leg.
- **Regression:** two-legged glute bridge.

### 8. Single-leg Romanian deadlift (bodyweight)
- **Equipment:** none; dip bar for a light fingertip balance at first (optional)
- **How:** Stand on one leg with a soft knee. Hinge forward at the hip as the free leg extends behind you, keeping your back flat and hips square, until you feel a hamstring stretch. Return to standing.
- **Common faults:** rounding the back; hips opening to the side; bending the standing knee to go lower.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12 each leg with no balance support.
- **Regression:** hold a dip bar for balance, or do a kickstand version with the back toes touching the floor.

### 9. Nordic curl (eccentric)
- **Equipment:** feet anchored securely under a heavy sofa or held by a partner (optional); cushion under the knees (optional)
- **How:** Kneel with your ankles anchored and your body straight from knees to head. Lower your body toward the floor as slowly as possible using your hamstrings. Catch yourself with your hands in a push-up position and push back up.
- **Common faults:** bending at the hips to shorten the lever; dropping fast in the first few degrees; an anchor that shifts.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 lowerings with a controlled descent most of the way down. Then add a pull back up from the bottom of the range. This is the top of the hinge line.
- **Regression:** a shorter range (stop partway and return), or single-leg Romanian deadlifts.

## Safety notes

- Knees: pain at or around the kneecap on deep single-leg work means regress the range for a few weeks. Don't push through it.
- Nordic curls cause significant muscle soreness at first. Start with one or two sets, and make sure the anchor can't move. A sliding sofa can mean a face-first fall.
- Use a handhold for balance on new single-leg rungs. A fall from a pistol is a worse outcome than a slower progression.
