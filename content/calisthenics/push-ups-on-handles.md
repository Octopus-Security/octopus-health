# Progression Ladder: Push-ups on Handles

Push-up handles do two useful things. They keep your wrists neutral, which is
easier on them than flat palms. They also lift your hands, so your chest can go
below hand level for a deeper deficit push-up. Assumption: the handles are
stable parallettes-style handles (see the section README). If yours rock or
spin, practise on a firm surface and progress more cautiously.

## The ladder

### 1. Incline push-up
- **Equipment:** dip bars (hands on the bars) or push-up handles on a stable raised surface
- **How:** Hands on the bars, body straight from head to heels, and lower your chest to the bars. The higher the bars, the easier the push-up.
- **Common faults:** hips sagging or piking; elbows flared straight out to 90°; half reps.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12 at a height where your body is close to 45° or lower.
- **Regression:** a higher incline, or a wall push-up.

### 2. Full push-up on handles
- **Equipment:** push-up handles
- **How:** Place the handles under your shoulders. Keep your body rigid with glutes and abs lightly braced. Lower until your chest is level with the handles, elbows at roughly 45° from your body. Press to full lockout with your shoulder blades spread slightly at the top.
- **Common faults:** head jutting forward; hips dropping; not locking out.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12.
- **Regression:** incline push-ups on the dip bars, or knee push-ups on the handles.

### 3. Deficit push-up on handles
- **Equipment:** push-up handles
- **How:** Same set-up, but lower past the handles so your chest drops below hand level. Go only as deep as your shoulders are comfortable. Pause briefly at the bottom.
- **Common faults:** bouncing out of the stretch; losing body tension to reach depth.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10 at full controlled depth.
- **Regression:** full push-ups with a 3-second lowering.

### 4. Archer push-up on handles
- **Equipment:** push-up handles
- **How:** Set the handles wide. Lower toward one hand while the other arm straightens out to the side as an assist. Press back up, and alternate sides.
- **Common faults:** the assisting arm bending and sharing the load; hips twisting.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 6 each side.
- **Regression:** a narrower archer with more help from the assisting arm, or deficit push-ups.

### 5. Pseudo-planche push-up on handles
- **Equipment:** push-up handles
- **How:** Place the handles beside your lower ribs or waist rather than your shoulders. Lean your shoulders forward past your hands, and keep that lean while doing push-ups. Keep your arms close and your body rigid.
- **Common faults:** the lean disappearing as you tire, so it turns into a normal push-up; wrists or elbows complaining (see safety notes).
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 while keeping the forward lean.
- **Regression:** less lean (handles closer to the shoulders), or a pseudo-planche lean hold without the push-up.

### 6. One-arm push-up progressions
- **Equipment:** dip bars (incline), then push-up handles or the floor
- **How:** Work in this order:
  1. Incline one-arm push-ups with a hand on a dip bar, feet wide.
  2. One-arm negatives on the floor or a handle.
  3. Full one-arm push-ups.

  Keep your feet wide and your hips square to the floor.
- **Common faults:** rotating your chest open to cheat; the hips twisting; not keeping your elbow close.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 each side on the incline before lowering the height, and 3 × 3 controlled one-arm negatives before full reps. This is the top of the ladder.
- **Regression:** archer push-ups.

## Safety notes

- Pseudo-planche push-ups load the wrists, elbows and front shoulders heavily. Add lean gradually and keep the volume low at first.
- Handles that roll or tip can cause a fall. Check they are stable on the surface you're using.
- Deficit depth is individual. Stop at the depth your shoulders tolerate without pain or pinching.
