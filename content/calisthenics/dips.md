# Progression Ladder: Dips

Parallel-bar dips build the chest, triceps and front shoulders, and they load
the shoulder hard at the bottom. **Depth is the main safety variable:** go
down only as far as you can control with your shoulders staying down and back.
For most people that means the upper arm about parallel to the floor. Go deeper
only if it is comfortable and controlled.

### A note on bench dips

Bench dips (hands behind you on a bench or chair, feet on the floor) are often
given as the beginner version. They're **not** used as a rung here. They put
the shoulder in a combined reached-back, rotated-in position that many people
find uncomfortable. The support hold and negatives below build the same
strength on the bars you'll use anyway. If you do use them, keep the range short
and stop at any front-of-shoulder discomfort.

## The ladder

### 1. Support hold
- **Equipment:** dip bars
- **How:** Jump or step up to straight arms on the bars. Press your shoulders down away from your ears, lock your elbows, and keep your body still with your legs slightly in front. Hold.
- **Common faults:** shrugging, where the shoulders sink toward the ears; soft, bent elbows; swinging.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 30-second holds with your shoulders depressed throughout.
- **Regression:** keep your toes lightly on a box or chair (optional), or do shorter holds.

### 2. Negative dip
- **Equipment:** dip bars
- **How:** From the support hold, lower slowly to your controlled depth in 3–5 seconds. Put your feet down, step back up, and repeat.
- **Common faults:** dropping into the bottom; shoulders rolling forward at depth; going deeper than you can control.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 lowerings of 5 seconds each, reaching the target depth.
- **Regression:** a shorter range (stop at a partial depth), or return to support holds.

### 3. Feet-assisted dip
- **Equipment:** dip bars; chair or box behind or under you (optional)
- **How:** Do full dips with one or both feet resting lightly on the chair to take some of your weight. Reduce the help over time.
- **Common faults:** legs doing the work; cutting the depth.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 with light foot pressure.
- **Regression:** negatives.

### 4. Full dip
- **Equipment:** dip bars
- **How:** From the support hold, lower with a slight forward lean and elbows tracking back, not flaring out. Stop at your controlled depth, then press back up to locked elbows with your shoulders down.
- **Common faults:** elbows flaring; bouncing out of the bottom; shrugging at the top.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10 (then build toward 3 × 12–15 before the next rung).
- **Regression:** feet-assisted dips for the later sets.

### 5. Weighted dip (optional load)
- **Equipment:** dip bars; backpack with books or water bottles (optional)
- **How:** Do the same full dip wearing a packed backpack. Add small amounts of load at a time.
- **Common faults:** adding load that forces you into a shallower or sloppier depth; the pack swinging.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 with the added load. Then add a little more load.
- **Regression:** remove the load and go back to bodyweight dips.

### 6. Russian dip (caution)
- **Equipment:** dip bars
- **How:** Lower into a dip, then continue down onto your forearms so they rest on the bars. Press back up onto your hands and out of the dip. This is an advanced movement that loads the shoulder at the very end of its range.
- **Common faults:** crashing onto the forearms; attempting it before full dips are strong; losing shoulder position at the transition.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 smooth transitions. This is the top of this ladder; progress with reps and control.
- **Regression:** full dips with a pause at the bottom.

### About the Korean dip

The Korean dip is done on a single straight bar behind your back. It puts the
shoulder in deep extension and is one of the harder dip variants on the
shoulders. With this equipment it's only possible if your pull-up bar can be set
at about hip height. Treat it as optional and advanced: build solid Russian dips
first, start with a small range of motion, and skip it entirely if the front of
your shoulder complains.

## Safety notes

- Depth: control it. Deep dips with rounded, forward-rolled shoulders are the classic way to irritate the front of the shoulder.
- Warm up with support holds and a few shallow dips before your working sets.
- Press to a full lockout with the shoulders down at the top of every rep. Hanging in a shrug loads the joint passively.
- Stop and regress for sharp or pinching pain at the front of the shoulder or sternum area.
- Check that the dip station is stable and can't walk or tip under you.
