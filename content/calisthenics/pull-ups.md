# Progression Ladder: Pull-ups

Overhand grip (palms facing away), hands about shoulder-width or a little
wider. Underhand work is in `chin-ups.md`. For the standards, see the section
README.

## The ladder

### 1. Dead hang
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** Grip the bar fully, thumbs wrapped. Hang with your arms straight and feet off the floor. Breathe. Let your shoulders rise toward your ears in a relaxed (passive) hang, then practise an active hang: draw the shoulders slightly down, away from your ears, without bending your arms.
- **Common faults:** gripping with the fingertips only; kicking or swinging; holding your breath.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 30-second holds, including 10 seconds in an active hang.
- **Regression:** keep your toes on a chair (optional) or the floor to take some weight, and build time.

### 2. Scapular pull-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** From a dead hang, keep your arms straight and pull your shoulder blades down and slightly back, so your body rises a few centimetres. Pause for 1 second, then lower back to a full hang.
- **Common faults:** bending your elbows to cheat the height; rushing the lowering; shrugging instead of depressing.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10 with a 1-second pause at the top.
- **Regression:** do the same movement with your feet on a chair (optional) or the floor.

### 3. Flexed-arm hang
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; chair or box to step up (optional)
- **How:** Step or jump to the top position with your chin over the bar, chest close, shoulders down. Hold it.
- **Common faults:** tipping your chin up to "hook" the bar; shoulders shrugged to your ears.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 20-second holds.
- **Regression:** hold with the bar at forehead height, or with your elbows at 90° instead of fully bent.

### 4. Negative pull-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; chair or box to step up (optional)
- **How:** Start in the top position and lower yourself slowly and evenly to a dead hang. Step back up; don't pull. Aim for 5 seconds or more per lowering.
- **Common faults:** dropping through the bottom half; letting the shoulders shrug at the bottom; doing so many that your elbows ache.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 lowerings, each lasting 5 seconds, evenly controlled all the way down.
- **Regression:** 3-second lowerings, or go back to the flexed-arm hang.

### 5. Feet-assisted pull-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; chair or box (optional), placed in front of or under you
- **How:** With one or both feet on the chair, do a full pull-up and use your legs only as much as needed. Use less leg help each session. This gives band-free assistance.
- **Common faults:** letting your legs do the work; cutting the range at the bottom.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 with light foot pressure throughout.
- **Regression:** push more with your legs, or return to negatives.

### 6. Full pull-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** From an active dead hang, pull your elbows down toward your ribs until your chin clears the bar. Lower to straight arms. Keep your legs still: straight and together, or crossed and slightly in front.
- **Common faults:** half reps (not reaching straight arms); kicking; craning your chin over the bar.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 strict (then build toward 3 × 10–12 before the next rung).
- **Regression:** feet-assisted pull-ups for the last sets, or sets of 1–3 reps with negatives added.

### 7. L-sit pull-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** Hold your legs straight out in front at hip height (an L-sit), then do strict pull-ups without letting your legs drop.
- **Common faults:** legs sinking during the pull; swinging to get started.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 6 with your legs held at or near horizontal.
- **Regression:** a tucked L (knees bent to your chest) while pulling.

### 8. Archer pull-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** Use a wide grip. Pull toward one hand while the other arm straightens out along the bar, acting as the assist. Lower, then alternate sides.
- **Common faults:** the "straight" arm bending and doing half the work; twisting the torso.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 each side.
- **Regression:** let the assisting arm bend more, or use a narrower grip.

### 9. Typewriter pull-up
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** Pull to the top with a wide grip, then slide across to one hand and back to the other while staying at the top. Lower from the centre.
- **Common faults:** dropping height as you traverse; rushing.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 4 traverses (to each side and back = 1).
- **Regression:** archer pull-ups, or pause at each side without traversing.

### 10. One-arm pull-up progressions
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar; towel (optional)
- **How:** Work in this order:
  1. One-arm negatives, lowering slowly from the top on a single arm.
  2. Assisted one-arm pull-ups, with the free hand holding a towel over the bar lower down.
  3. Full one-arm pull-ups.
- **Common faults:** spinning away from the bar; jumping into it before archers are solid; excessive volume on the elbow.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 3 controlled one-arm negatives of 5 seconds each side before attempting full reps. This is the top of the ladder, so progress from here by adding reps.
- **Regression:** typewriter or archer pull-ups.

## Safety notes

- The elbows take the brunt of high pull-up volume. If the inside or outside of your elbow aches, reduce sets, vary your grip width, and regress a rung. Don't push through it.
- Doorway bars: never pull outward or swing, and check the bar is seated before every session.
- Get down safely: step down or lower from a dead hang. Don't drop from the top.
- One-arm work puts a heavy load on a single elbow and shoulder. Use it sparingly, a few quality sets, never to failure.
