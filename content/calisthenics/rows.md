# Progression Ladder: Rows

Rows balance all the pressing in the other ladders and protect the shoulders.
**Bar height decides what you can do:**

- **Dip bars at waist-to-chest height** are usually the best row station here. Lie underneath, grip one bar in each hand with a neutral grip (palms facing each other), and row between them. Only do this if the station is heavy or anchored and rated for your weight. A light freestanding station can tip when you pull on it from underneath. Test it with a gentle pull first.
- **A doorway pull-up bar** is usually too high for anything but a steep, nearly upright row. If your bar is on a rack or can be mounted low, set it at about hip height and treat it like the dip-bar version.
- A sturdy table edge is sometimes suggested. If you use one (optional), make sure it cannot tip or slide.

## The ladder

### 1. Steep incline row
- **Equipment:** dip bars or a low-set pull-up bar
- **How:** Hold the bar(s) with arms straight, feet close under the bar, and body leaning back at a steep angle, straight from head to heels. Pull your chest to the bar and squeeze your shoulder blades together. Lower under control.
- **Common faults:** hips sagging; pulling with the neck and shrugging; not reaching full arm extension at the bottom.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12.
- **Regression:** stand more upright, closer to vertical.

### 2. Incline row, lower angle
- **Equipment:** dip bars or a low-set pull-up bar
- **How:** Walk your feet further forward so your body is closer to horizontal. Keep the same cues.
- **Common faults:** bending at the hips to shorten the lever; half reps.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12 with your body at roughly 45° or lower.
- **Regression:** return to the steep incline row.

### 3. Inverted row, knees bent
- **Equipment:** dip bars or a hip-height pull-up bar
- **How:** Lie under the bar(s), body nearly horizontal, knees bent and feet flat. Keep a straight line from knees to shoulders. Row your chest to the bar.
- **Common faults:** hips dropping at the bottom; elbows flaring straight out.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10.
- **Regression:** a lower-angle incline row.

### 4. Inverted row, legs straight
- **Equipment:** dip bars or a hip-height pull-up bar
- **How:** As above, with your legs straight and heels on the floor. Your body is a rigid plank.
- **Common faults:** piking at the hips; not touching the bar.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10, with a 1-second squeeze at the top.
- **Regression:** inverted row with knees bent.

### 5. Feet-elevated inverted row
- **Equipment:** dip bars or a hip-height pull-up bar; chair or box for the feet (optional)
- **How:** Put your heels up on the chair so your body is horizontal or slightly head-down. Row as before.
- **Common faults:** the chair sliding (put it against a wall); hips sagging.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10.
- **Regression:** inverted row with legs straight and feet on the floor.

### 6. Archer row
- **Equipment:** a hip-height pull-up bar (dip bars are usually too narrow for this)
- **How:** Use a wide grip. Row toward one hand while the other arm stays straight along the bar. Alternate sides.
- **Common faults:** the straight arm bending; the torso rotating.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 6 each side. Beyond this, the next step is a one-arm row on the same set-up.
- **Regression:** feet-elevated inverted rows.

## Safety notes

- Check the station before every set. Pulling up on a dip station from below is exactly the load that makes a light one tip.
- Keep your neck neutral. Lead with your chest, not your chin.
- If you can't do rows safely with your equipment, use extra pull-up and chin-up volume and add scapular pull-ups (see `pull-ups.md`) for shoulder balance.
