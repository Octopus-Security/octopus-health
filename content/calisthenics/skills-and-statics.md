# Progression Ladder: Skills and Statics

These are mostly **straight-arm** skills. They ask a lot of the tendons in the
elbows, biceps and shoulders, and those adapt more slowly than muscle. The
rules for this whole file:

- Train skills **fresh**, at the start of a session, after a warm-up.
- Keep holds **short and good** (5–15 seconds), with several sets, and stop before your form fails.
- Train them **2–3 times a week** at most, not daily.
- **Ramp up gradually** when you start a new rung. Don't jump straight to max-effort holds.

**Bar clearance:** levers need your body to fit horizontally under the bar,
and muscle-ups need room above it. Most doorway bars allow neither. Use a
rack or a wall- or ceiling-mounted bar with space around it, or stick to the
support and planche lines.

## The ladder

### 1. Support hold progressions
- **Equipment:** dip bars; push-up handles
- **How:** Hold a straight-arm support (see the dips ladder) on the dip bars, then on the handles, with your shoulders depressed and elbows locked. On the handles, practise leaning slightly forward and back while holding the lockout.
- **Common faults:** bent elbows; shrugging; holding your breath.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 30-second holds on the handles with your shoulders depressed.
- **Regression:** shorter holds, or keep your toes lightly on the floor.

### 2. Tuck front lever
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar with room for your body horizontally below it
- **How:** From an active hang with arms straight, pull your shoulder blades down and push the bar toward your hips. Lift into a tight tuck (knees to chest) so your back is horizontal and facing the floor, hips level with your shoulders. Hold.
- **Common faults:** bending your arms; hips sagging below your shoulders; rounding into a ball rather than holding a flat back.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 15-second holds with a flat back and straight arms. Then move to an advanced tuck (back flat, hips opened to about 90°), then one-leg, then straddle. Treat these as long-term goals.
- **Regression:** tuck front lever negatives (lower slowly from an inverted hang), or active hangs with scapular depression.

### 3. Back lever progressions
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar with room above and below; a spotter or a practised bail (recommended)
- **How:** From a hang, tuck and rotate backwards through an inverted position, lowering into a German hang (the arms behind you in a stretch). This is called skin-the-cat. Then return. Once skin-the-cat is comfortable, stop halfway on the way down in a tuck, facing the floor, with your body horizontal. That's the tuck back lever.
- **Common faults:** dropping into the German hang uncontrolled; bent arms; too much volume too soon.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 5 slow skin-the-cats, then 3 × 10-second tuck back lever holds. Progress from there to an advanced tuck, then one-leg.
- **Regression:** a partial skin-the-cat that stops before the German hang, or tuck holds with your feet lightly on a chair (optional).

### 4. Crow pose on handles
- **Equipment:** push-up handles
- **How:** Squat with your hands on the handles. Place your knees on the backs of your upper arms, lean forward until your feet lift off, and balance.
- **Common faults:** looking down at your feet (look slightly forward); not leaning far enough; no cushion for a nose-dive.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 20-second holds.
- **Regression:** lift one foot at a time.

### 5. Tuck planche on handles
- **Equipment:** push-up handles
- **How:** Lock your arms on the handles and lean forward. Round your upper back (protract your shoulder blades) and lift your tucked knees off the floor, knees away from the arms, holding only with straight arms. Your hips should rise to about shoulder height.
- **Common faults:** bent arms (turning it into a crow); the upper back caving in; not leaning far enough.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10-second holds. Then work toward an advanced tuck (back flat) over the long term.
- **Regression:** a crow pose, or pseudo-planche lean holds with your feet on the floor.

### 6. Muscle-up prerequisites
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar with room above it; dip bars
- **How:** Before trying muscle-ups, build these:
  - Strict pull-ups.
  - Explosive high pull-ups that bring your chest to the bar.
  - Straight-bar dips: jump to the top of the pull-up bar in support, then dip and press. If your bar can't support this, do full parallel-bar dips instead.
- **Common faults:** attempting muscle-ups on pulling strength alone; high pull-ups that stop at the chin.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10 strict pull-ups, 3 × 5 chest-to-bar pull-ups, and 3 × 10 dips (straight-bar or parallel). These are common coaching benchmarks, not hard rules.
- **Regression:** back to full pull-ups and full dips.

### 7. Muscle-up progression
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar with room above; a chair or box (optional) for jumping assistance if the bar is low
- **How:** Work in this order:
  1. **Negatives:** lower from the top support, through the transition, into a hang.
  2. **Jumping muscle-ups:** jump from a box through the transition.
  3. **Strict muscle-ups:** pull explosively toward your lower chest, lean your chest over the bar, turn your wrists over, and press into a dip.
- **Common faults:** a chicken-wing, where one elbow comes over first and takes the load unevenly; pulling to the chin rather than the chest; heavy kipping before strict strength exists.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 3 strict muscle-ups. This is the top of the ladder; progress with reps and control.
- **Regression:** muscle-up negatives and chest-to-bar pull-ups.

## Safety notes

- **Straight-arm load:** biceps-tendon and elbow pain is the classic sign of too much front lever, back lever or planche volume too soon. Cut volume and regress before it becomes a lasting problem.
- **Back lever and German hang:** these put the shoulder into deep extension. Enter them slowly and with control, never by dropping. Skip them if you have a history of shoulder dislocation or instability, unless a clinician has cleared you.
- **Planche and crow:** put a cushion in front of you for nose-dives. Watch your wrists; the handles help keep them neutral.
- **Muscle-up transitions** load the elbows and shoulders quickly. Learn them strict before adding kip.
- **Bar and room:** clear space around the bar; check the bar can take dynamic load. A doorway bar should not be used for levers or muscle-ups.
