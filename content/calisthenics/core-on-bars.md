# Progression Ladder: Core on Bars

This file has two lines.

- **The hanging line** uses the pull-up bar and trains the abs through hip flexion while you hang.
- **The L-sit line** uses the dip bars or handles. It's a support hold that trains compression, meaning the abs and hip flexors pulling your legs toward your torso.

If your grip gives out before your abs do on the hanging line, do the knee
raises in dip-bar support (captain's-chair style) while your grip catches up.

## The ladder

### 1. Hanging knee raise
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar (or dip bars in support, as the captain's-chair version)
- **How:** From an active hang, curl your knees up toward your chest, tilting your pelvis so your lower back rounds slightly at the top. Lower slowly without swinging.
- **Common faults:** swinging to lift the legs; only lifting the thighs without curling the pelvis; dropping the legs.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 12 with no swing.
- **Regression:** the captain's-chair version on dip bars, or lying knee tucks on the floor.

### 2. Hanging leg raise
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar
- **How:** Keep your legs straight and together. Raise them to hip height (90°) or higher, then lower under control.
- **Common faults:** bending your knees to cheat; momentum from the bottom; arching your lower back.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 10 to at least horizontal.
- **Regression:** hanging knee raises, or bent-knee raises with the legs straightening at the top.

### 3. Toes-to-bar
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar (needs clearance in front of the bar for your legs, so a doorway bar may not allow the full range)
- **How:** Raise your straight legs all the way until your toes touch the bar, pressing down through straight arms to help your hips rise. Lower slowly and keep your body still between reps.
- **Common faults:** kipping; swinging between reps; bending your knees heavily.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 8 strict.
- **Regression:** hanging leg raises to as high as you can control.

### 4. Windshield wiper
- **Equipment:** pull-up bar (needs clearance to both sides)
- **How:** Raise your legs to the bar (or as close as you can) and hold them there. Rotate them from side to side in an arc, like a windshield wiper. Control the rotation and keep your hips high.
- **Common faults:** legs dropping on each pass; twisting only as far as momentum allows.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 6 each side (side to side and back = 1). This is the top of the hanging line.
- **Regression:** half-range wipers with bent knees, or lying windshield wipers on the floor.

### 5. Tuck L-sit
- **Equipment:** dip bars or push-up handles
- **How:** Press into a support hold with arms locked and shoulders pushed down. Lift your knees into your chest, feet off the floor. Hold.
- **Common faults:** shrugging; bent elbows; feet touching down.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 20-second holds.
- **Regression:** support hold with one foot lightly touching the floor, or shorter holds.

### 6. One-leg L-sit
- **Equipment:** dip bars or push-up handles
- **How:** From the tuck, extend one leg straight out in front at hip height, keeping the other tucked. Swap legs between holds.
- **Common faults:** the straight leg drooping; the hips sinking behind the hands.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 15-second holds on each leg.
- **Regression:** tuck L-sit.

### 7. Full L-sit
- **Equipment:** dip bars or push-up handles
- **How:** Hold both legs straight and together at hip height, toes pointed, arms locked and shoulders down.
- **Common faults:** bent knees; legs below horizontal; rounded, shrugged shoulders.
- **Move up when:** 3 × 15-second holds, then build toward 30 seconds. Beyond this sits the V-sit, where the legs rise above horizontal. It takes much more compression strength and hamstring flexibility, and is a long-term goal.
- **Regression:** one-leg L-sit, or alternate bending one knee through the hold.

## Safety notes

- On the hanging line, stay out of shrugged, passive hangs if they bother your shoulders. Use an active hang.
- Bar clearance: toes-to-bar and windshield wipers need room. Don't do them on a doorway bar where your feet could hit the frame or wall.
- L-sits load the wrists and elbows in straight-arm support. The handles and dip bars keep the wrists neutral, but ramp up hold times gradually.
- Hip flexor or lower-back pain in L-sits usually means holding too long too soon. Return to shorter tuck holds.
